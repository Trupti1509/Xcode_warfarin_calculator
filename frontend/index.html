Clinical Use

This calculator provides an estimate of the expected stable warfarin maintenance dose for adults with a target INR of 2.0–3.0.

The calculated weekly dose should be interpreted together with the patient's clinical circumstances and translated by the treating clinician into an appropriate practical dosing regimen. Subsequent warfarin dosing should be individualized according to INR response, indication, interacting medications, diet, comorbidities, and other relevant clinical factors.

The calculated estimate does not replace INR monitoring or clinical judgment.

Frequently Asked Questions & Methodology

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What does this calculator estimate?

This calculator estimates an expected stable warfarin maintenance dose in milligrams per week and provides the corresponding average daily dose. It is intended for adult patients with a target INR of 2.0–3.0. The result is a dose estimate and is not a prescription, loading-dose recommendation, or INR-based dose adjustment.

2. Which dosing algorithm does the calculator use?

The calculator uses the published International Warfarin Pharmacogenetics Consortium (IWPC) dosing algorithms. Two calculation pathways are available:

  • Pharmacogenetic IWPC algorithm: Uses clinical information together with suitable CYP2C9 and VKORC1 genetic information.
  • Clinical IWPC algorithm: Uses clinical information alone when suitable pharmacogenetic information is unavailable or does not meet the requirements for the PGx pathway.

The calculator identifies which pathway was used for each result.

3. What clinical information is used in the calculation?

The IWPC algorithm uses:

  • Age
  • Height
  • Weight
  • Self-identified population category
  • Amiodarone use
  • Use of specific enzyme-inducing medications included in the IWPC model

For the enzyme-inducer variable, the IWPC model includes carbamazepine, phenytoin, and rifampin/rifampicin. These variables are part of the published dosing equation and should not be interpreted as a complete assessment of all factors that can influence warfarin response.

4. Which genetic results are used?

The core IWPC pharmacogenetic calculation uses:

  • CYP2C9 *2 and *3
  • VKORC1 c.-1639G>A (rs9923231)

Additional variants may be considered separately according to CPIC guidance:

  • CYP2C9 *5, *6, *8, and *11
  • CYP4F2 rs2108622
  • CYP2C rs12777823

These additional findings are displayed separately rather than automatically incorporated into the original IWPC equation.

5. What happens if a required genetic result is missing?

A missing genetic result is not assumed to be normal. If the genetic information required for the pharmacogenetic calculation is unavailable or unsuitable, the calculator uses the published IWPC clinical dosing algorithm when appropriate. The result screen identifies when clinical-only dosing has been used and explains why.

6. Why is self-identified population information requested?

Population information is one of the variables included in the published IWPC equations. It is also relevant to the implementation of CPIC pharmacogenetic guidance because the frequencies and clinical importance of some CYP2C9 variants differ among ancestry groups. Population category must be entered from the patient's clinical information. It is not inferred from the patient's DNA by this calculator.

7. Why are CYP2C9 *5, *6, *8, and *11 important for patients of African ancestry?

CYP2C9 *5, *6, *8, and *11 are reduced-function alleles that occur more commonly in populations of African ancestry. CPIC recommends that these alleles be considered when genotype-guided warfarin dosing is used for patients of African ancestry. If adequate testing for these alleles is not available, this calculator does not assume they are absent and instead uses the clinical dosing pathway.

8. What is rs12777823 and when is it used?

rs12777823 is a variant in the CYP2C gene region associated with warfarin dose requirements in African Americans. When the relevant population context and genotype information are available, CPIC provides an additional dose-reduction recommendation for carriers of the A allele. This consideration is displayed separately from the original IWPC dose estimate.

9. What role does CYP4F2 play?

The CYP4F2 rs2108622 variant can modestly influence warfarin dose requirements. For appropriate patients, CPIC allows an additional dose-increase consideration for carriers of the CYP4F2*3-associated allele. Because CYP4F2 is not part of the original IWPC equation, this calculator displays the CPIC consideration separately rather than silently modifying the IWPC result.

10. Why are some CPIC adjustments shown separately from the calculated dose?

The original IWPC equation was developed using a specific set of clinical and genetic variables. CPIC subsequently provided additional recommendations for variants including CYP2C9 *5, *6, *8, *11, CYP4F2, and rs12777823. To preserve the published IWPC calculation, this calculator reports these additional CPIC considerations separately rather than combining them automatically into a new, unpublished equation.

11. Does the calculator provide a safe dose range?

No. The IWPC algorithm produces an estimated stable maintenance dose. The calculated value should not be interpreted as an exact dose or as a patient-specific safe range. Warfarin has substantial variability between individuals, and the appropriate dose must ultimately be determined using INR response and clinical assessment.

12. Does the calculated daily dose represent a tablet schedule?

No. The daily value shown is the calculated weekly dose divided by seven and represents an average daily equivalent. It does not specify tablet strength, alternating-day dosing, or a prescribed weekly tablet schedule. The treating clinician should determine the practical dosing regimen.

13. Can this calculator be used to adjust a patient's current dose based on today's INR?

No. This version estimates an expected stable maintenance dose using the IWPC algorithms. It does not calculate dose changes from a current INR, previous warfarin doses, or recent treatment response. Ongoing warfarin management should follow INR-guided clinical protocols.

14. Does this calculator replace INR monitoring?

No. Genetic and clinical information can help estimate warfarin dose requirements, but they do not replace INR monitoring. Warfarin dosing must continue to be individualized according to INR response, indication, interacting medications, dietary factors, comorbidities, and other relevant clinical information.

15. Can results derived from consumer raw DNA be used directly to change treatment?

Consumer raw DNA files may have incomplete genetic-marker coverage and are not equivalent to comprehensive clinical pharmacogenetic testing. Genetic findings intended to influence prescribing or dosing should be reviewed by the treating clinician and, when appropriate, confirmed using a validated clinical pharmacogenetic test before treatment decisions are made.

16. What does CYP2C9 *1 mean?

CYP2C9 *1 generally indicates that no defining alternative allele was identified at the relevant positions that were available for analysis. When the source is a consumer raw DNA file, not every position required to characterize CYP2C9 may have been tested. Therefore, a *1 result should not be interpreted as proof that the entire gene was sequenced or that all possible reduced-function variants were excluded.

17. What should a clinician do with the calculated result?

Use the result as one source of information when estimating warfarin maintenance requirements. Before applying it clinically, review: the patient's indication and target INR, the genetic-test source and marker coverage, interacting medications and other clinical factors, and the patient's INR response. The final warfarin regimen and subsequent dose adjustments should remain under clinician supervision.

IWPC Published Equations

IWPC Clinical Dosing Equation: √ weekly dose = 4.0376 − 0.2546 × age in decades + 0.0118 × height (cm) + 0.0134 × weight (kg) − 0.6752 × Asian + 0.4060 × Black/African American + 0.0443 × Mixed/Unknown + 1.2799 × enzyme inducer − 0.5695 × amiodarone
IWPC Pharmacogenetic Dosing Equation (Original 2009 NEJM Model): √ weekly dose = 5.6044 − 0.2614 × age in decades + 0.0087 × height (cm) + 0.0128 × weight (kg) − 0.8677 × VKORC1 G/A − 1.6974 × VKORC1 A/A − 0.4854 × VKORC1 genotype unknown − 0.5211 × CYP2C9 *1/*2 − 0.9357 × CYP2C9 *1/*3 − 1.0616 × CYP2C9 *2/*2 − 1.9206 × CYP2C9 *2/*3 − 2.3312 × CYP2C9 *3/*3 − 0.2188 × CYP2C9 genotype unknown − 0.1092 × Asian − 0.2760 × Black/African American − 0.1032 × Mixed/Unknown + 1.1816 × enzyme inducer − 0.5503 × amiodarone

Implementation note on unknown genotype coefficients: The published 2009 IWPC pharmacogenetic equation included coefficients for unknown VKORC1 (−0.4854) and unknown CYP2C9 (−0.2188). Xcode Life does not apply these unknown-genotype coefficients. When genotype data are incomplete or unavailable, Xcode Life instead uses the dedicated IWPC clinical dosing algorithm, consistent with CPIC implementation guidance.

The equation value is squared to obtain the estimated weekly dose in mg/week. Dividing by 7 provides the average daily equivalent in mg/day. Age in decades is binned as min(floor(age / 10), 9), where age 90+ is binned as 9.
